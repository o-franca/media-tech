# Vibe Design: How Marketers Are Using Claude Code, GitHub and Canva to Ship Ads at Speed

A year ago, "vibe coding" entered the vocabulary of every founder on X. You describe what you want in plain English, an AI agent writes the code, you keep refining until the thing works. The result was a wave of solo developers shipping products that would have needed a team of five in 2023.

Something almost identical is now happening in marketing. Small teams across Ireland and the UK are quietly building a parallel workflow that produces ads, social posts, reels and full campaigns in a fraction of the time a traditional studio would need. People are calling it vibe design, and the stack is unusual: Claude Code, GitHub, Canva, plus a handful of AI video tools stitched together with prompts and patience.

I have been running this exact stack with clients for the last few months. The output is not theoretical. It is real campaigns going live for real businesses. Here is how it works, why it matters, and where it falls down.

## What Vibe Design Actually Means

Vibe design is the application of the same loose, conversational, agentic workflow that powered vibe coding to creative production. Instead of writing software, you are writing assets: ad creative, captions, scripts, reels, carousels, landing-page mockups, and the variations of all of those that a modern paid campaign demands.

The core idea is that the marketer becomes the director. You do not draw, animate, or edit frame by frame. You describe the brief, set the brand rules, hand the heavy lifting to AI tools, and spend your time judging the output and iterating on the bits that miss.

This is a meaningful shift. For decades, the bottleneck in small-business marketing was production capacity. You had ideas. You did not have the hands to execute them. Vibe design removes that constraint -- not entirely, but enough to change what a one-person marketing team can realistically deliver in a week.

## The Stack: Why These Specific Tools

Every team has a slightly different setup, but a pattern is emerging across the marketers I speak to. The four pillars look like this:

### 1. Claude Code as the Creative Operations Layer

Claude Code started life as a developer tool -- an agentic coding assistant that lives in your terminal. Marketers picked it up for a reason that surprised even Anthropic: it is genuinely good at orchestrating creative work.

What people are actually doing with it:

- **Generating ad copy variations at scale.** Feed it your brand voice document, your product page, and your three top-performing ads. Ask for fifty variations targeted at different audience segments. You get them in a structured file you can drop straight into Meta Ads Manager.
- **Writing scripts for short-form video.** UGC-style scripts, hook-driven Reels, voiceovers for TikTok. Because Claude Code can read your existing scripts and study what worked, the output is grounded in your actual performance data, not a generic template.
- **Building campaign briefs.** A marketer types "We are launching the new accountancy package next month, target small Irish businesses, budget five thousand euro, tone is professional but warm." The agent produces a brief, channel mix, asset list, and content calendar in a single file.
- **Automating the boring bits.** Renaming exports, generating UTM links, writing meta descriptions for every blog post in a backlog, producing CSV files for bulk uploads.

The reason Claude Code specifically -- rather than a chat interface -- is that it can read and write files, run scripts, and operate on a whole project folder. You stop copying and pasting. The agent works on your campaign the way a junior creative would, except it never gets tired and it remembers everything you have already approved.

### 2. GitHub as the Brand Source of Truth

This is the part that confuses people who have never worked in software. Why would a marketing team use GitHub?

Because brand assets, ad copy, scripts, and campaigns are all just files. And GitHub is the best system humans have invented for managing files that change over time, especially when more than one person is touching them.

In a vibe design workflow, a typical repository looks like this:

- A folder of brand guidelines: tone of voice, do-not-say list, approved colour codes, logos, fonts.
- A folder of past campaigns, organised by channel and date, so the AI can study what has worked.
- A folder of templates: Meta ad headlines, LinkedIn post structures, email sequences.
- A folder of in-progress work, where the AI drops new drafts.
- A folder of approved, ready-to-publish assets.

When a freelancer or agency partner joins, you give them access to the repo. They get the entire history of your brand and can ramp up in an afternoon. When you ask Claude Code to draft something new, it has full context. When something goes wrong, you can roll back to the version that worked.

For most small businesses in Ireland and the UK, this is overkill if you are running one campaign a quarter. But once you are producing twenty pieces of content a week, having a single source of truth is the difference between a smooth operation and chaos.

### 3. Canva as the Visual Production Layer

Canva is the workhorse of small-business design and has been for years. What has changed is its integration with AI. Magic Studio, Magic Resize, Magic Write, and the various AI-powered template tools mean that one person can now produce twenty platform-perfect ad variations in the time it used to take to produce one.

The workflow most teams settle on:

- Build a small library of master templates that match your brand: a square ad, a vertical reel cover, a LinkedIn carousel, a Story background. These are your skeletons.
- When a campaign brief comes out of Claude Code, drop the headlines and copy into the templates. Use Magic Resize to produce every required format in a click.
- Use Canva's AI image generation for backgrounds, illustrations, and abstract visuals. Reserve real photography for product shots and people -- AI photography of humans still falls into the uncanny valley too often to risk in a paid ad.
- Export in batches. Canva's bulk-export and Brand Kit features mean the file naming is consistent, which matters when you are uploading thirty assets to an ad platform.

For teams already using Figma for product work, the same logic applies. The point is not the specific tool. It is having one place where the brand is locked down and where production happens fast.

### 4. AI Video Tools for the Hard Part

Video is where vibe design earns its keep. Producing a thirty-second video ad used to mean a shoot, a script, an editor, music licensing, and a week of revisions. AI video tools collapse most of that into an afternoon.

The category is moving so fast that any specific tool I name today will look quaint in six months. What matters is the categories of tool and how they fit together:

- **AI video generation from text or image.** Produces short clips, b-roll, abstract visuals, and product animations from prompts. Useful for top-of-funnel hooks where the goal is to stop the scroll, not to communicate detailed information.
- **AI avatar and voiceover tools.** Generate a presenter delivering your script. Quality has improved enough that for explainer videos and LinkedIn ads, the result is broadcast-ready. For higher-trust content -- testimonials, founder stories -- real humans still win.
- **AI editing assistants.** Tools that take a long-form video (a podcast, a webinar, a Zoom recording) and automatically produce a dozen short-form clips with captions, hooks, and platform-specific framing. This is where most teams get the biggest leverage. One hour of source footage becomes a month of social content.
- **AI music and sound design.** Royalty-free, custom-generated soundtracks that match the mood of the video. No more searching stock libraries for forty-five minutes.

The vibe design move is to stitch these together with Claude Code in the middle, orchestrating the brief, the script, the asset list, and the upload pipeline. You write a prompt. You get a campaign.

## A Real Workflow: Launching a Campaign in a Day

Here is what a typical day looks like for a small business owner using this stack to launch a new campaign for a specific offer -- say, a Dublin physiotherapy clinic running a back-to-running programme for the summer.

**9:00 AM.** Open the GitHub repo. Pull the latest brand guidelines and last summer's campaign assets so they are fresh in mind.

**9:15 AM.** Open Claude Code. Type a brief: target audience, offer, budget, channels, deadlines, tone. Ask for a campaign plan, ad copy variations for Meta and Google, three Reel scripts, an email sequence, and a landing page outline.

**10:00 AM.** Review the output. Edit the bits that miss the brand voice. Approve the rest. Commit the approved files to the repo so the version is locked.

**10:30 AM.** Open Canva. Drop the approved headlines into your master ad templates. Use Magic Resize to generate every required size. Export.

**12:00 PM.** Lunch.

**1:00 PM.** Use an AI video tool to produce three Reel variations from the scripts. Generate b-roll for the running scenes. Use an AI voiceover for one of the variations to test against a self-recorded version.

**3:00 PM.** Review the videos. Pick the two best. Run them through an AI editing tool to add captions and adjust pacing for vertical viewing.

**4:00 PM.** Upload everything to Meta Ads Manager and Google Ads. Set up tracking. Launch.

**4:45 PM.** Write a one-page summary of what was produced and where it went. Drop it in the GitHub repo so future campaigns can learn from this one.

Six hours, end to end, for a campaign that would have taken a small agency two weeks and a four-figure invoice. The output is not better than what an experienced creative team would produce. It is competitive enough that the difference does not matter for most small businesses.

## Where Vibe Design Falls Down

I am not going to pretend this stack is a silver bullet. It has clear limits, and ignoring them will burn money.

### Brand Voice Drift

AI tools have a strong gravitational pull toward generic, safe language. If you do not feed them a tight brand voice document and edit aggressively, every campaign will sound like every other campaign. Teams who succeed at vibe design spend more time on their brand voice document than on any individual asset. The document is the leverage point.

### The Sameness Problem

If you can produce a campaign in a day, so can your competitors. Output volume is no longer a moat. The new moat is taste -- knowing which of the fifty AI-generated variations is the one worth running, and having the judgement to throw the other forty-nine away. This is where experienced marketers still beat anyone with a faster tool.

### Compliance and Rights

AI-generated images and video sit in a legal grey area that varies by jurisdiction. For Irish and UK businesses, the safer path is to use AI for backgrounds, abstract visuals, and stylised content, while sticking to real photography and real people for anything that depicts your product, your team, or specific claims. If a regulator ever questions an ad, "the AI made it" is not a defence.

### The Small-Business Trap

Some teams adopt vibe design and immediately try to produce twenty times more content. This usually backfires. Algorithms reward quality and engagement, not volume. A small business posting four excellent pieces a week beats one posting thirty mediocre ones. Use the time savings to be more strategic, not just more prolific.

## Why This Matters for Small Businesses in Ireland and the UK

The conventional wisdom for the last decade has been that small businesses cannot compete with bigger brands on production value. Vibe design changes the maths.

A two-person business in Cork can now run a campaign that looks and sounds like one from a thirty-person agency. A solo founder in Manchester can produce a launch video that two years ago would have required an external production company. The cost of looking professional has collapsed.

This does not mean every business should drop their agency tomorrow. Strategy, planning, media buying expertise, and creative direction are still genuinely difficult and worth paying for. What has changed is the production layer underneath. If you are paying an agency mostly for execution -- resizing ads, churning out variations, basic video editing -- you are paying for something a small in-house team with the right stack can now do faster.

## Getting Started Without Overcommitting

If you are reading this and thinking the whole setup sounds intimidating, the good news is you do not need to adopt all of it at once. The teams who get the most from vibe design started with one tool and added the others over months, not in a single weekend.

A reasonable first step for most small businesses looks like this:

1. **Pick a single channel** where you already produce content -- say, Instagram Reels or LinkedIn posts.
2. **Spend a week** writing a tight brand voice document. Tone, vocabulary, sentence rhythm, examples of good and bad copy. Two pages, no more.
3. **Use an AI tool** -- Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, whichever you prefer -- to draft the next month of content for that one channel, using your brand voice document as the brief.
4. **Edit aggressively.** Notice which parts of the output you keep and which you rewrite. That gap is the next thing to add to your brand voice document.
5. **Add a second tool** only when the first one feels easy. Maybe that is Canva for visual production. Maybe it is an AI video tool for converting your blog posts into Reels.

The teams who try to adopt the whole stack in a week usually give up by the second week. The teams who add one tool a month are running full campaigns by the end of the quarter.

## FAQ

### Do I need to know how to code to use Claude Code for marketing?

No. Claude Code is operated through plain-English instructions. You describe what you want, the agent does the work. If you can write a clear brief for a freelancer, you can use Claude Code. The benefit of it being a developer tool is that it can read and write files in your project folder, but you do not need to write any code yourself.

### Is GitHub really necessary for a small marketing team?

Not on day one. If you are a solo marketer producing a handful of assets a week, a well-organised cloud drive is fine. GitHub starts to pay off when more than one person is editing assets, when you want to track changes over time, or when you want to give an AI agent a single source of truth for your brand. Treat it as a tool you grow into, not a starting requirement.

### Can AI video tools replace a real production team?

For top-of-funnel and mid-funnel content -- Reels, TikTok hooks, simple explainer videos -- often yes. For brand films, customer testimonials, and high-trust content, real production still wins. The mistake is treating it as all-or-nothing. The smartest teams use AI video for the high-volume formats and reserve their production budget for the hero pieces that need to feel handcrafted.

### How do I stop AI-generated ads from looking generic?

Two things. First, invest serious time in a brand voice document and reference assets. Second, accept that the AI is producing first drafts, not finished work. The teams whose AI output looks generic are the teams who skip the editing step. Treat AI output the way a senior editor treats a junior writer's draft -- valuable starting material, never the final version.

### Is this approach GDPR-compliant?

The tools themselves can be used in a GDPR-compliant way, but you have to set them up correctly. Do not feed customer data, email lists, or personal information into AI tools without checking the data-handling terms. Most AI vendors offer business or enterprise tiers with stricter privacy guarantees. For Irish and UK businesses handling any personal data, those tiers are usually worth the extra cost.

## The Bottom Line

Vibe design is not a fad and it is not magic. It is a stack of tools, used carefully, that lets a small marketing team operate at the pace of a much larger one. The barrier to entry is no longer money or headcount. It is taste, judgement, and the willingness to learn a new way of working.

For small businesses in Ireland and the UK, this is the most significant change to the production side of marketing in twenty years. The teams who learn the workflow this year will spend the next decade out-producing the ones who wait.

Start with one tool. Write a tight brand voice document. Edit aggressively. The rest follows.
